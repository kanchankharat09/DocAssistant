PROJECT OVERVIEW: Patient History Assistant (S1 to S12)
=======================================================

Read this file to understand the whole project and to explain it to someone else.
The Hindi version is project_overview_hindi.txt. The state written here is from 27 Oct 2026.


1. THE PROJECT IN TWO MINUTES (you can say this aloud)
------------------------------------------------------
A doctor in a small clinic talks to a patient. We take the written conversation (a
transcript) and an AI turns it into a short structured summary: complaint, duration,
history, medicines with doses. The doctor reads the summary, fixes anything wrong and
presses Approve. Only then is it saved. At the next visit the doctor searches the patient
and sees a "recall card" with the last visit in a few seconds.
Between the AI and the doctor there is a checker. It flags a medicine name or a dose that
looks wrong, so a mistake does not get saved by accident.

Why we build it: repeat patients are common and a doctor has little time. We want to know
two things: (1) is the summary good and safe, and (2) is the recall card really faster than
reading the old transcript?

Everything is a test on mock data: 20 made-up transcripts, 20 made-up patients. No real
patient, no real recording.

The idea we test (the hypothesis, from step.md): "If we build this, then on 20 transcripts at
least 18 summaries keep every required detail, and role-playing doctors find the key details
on the card in a median under 15 seconds, at least 2x faster than reading the transcript."
We are wrong if the result is below that (for example the card is less than 2x faster, or a
wrong dose is saved without a flag).

3. EVERY STEP: WHAT, WHY, WHICH FILES, WHAT NUMBER
--------------------------------------------------
S1  Test data
    What:  20 transcripts and 20 "expected summaries" (the right answers). 5 transcripts
           mix Hindi and English. 10 contain one planted error (5 wrong medicine names,
           5 wrong doses). test_data/MANIFEST.csv lists them.
    Why:   Every test must be repeatable. This is the exam paper and the answer key.
    Files: test_data/, test_data/check_counts.py

S2  Fictional patients and database
    What:  A database (clinic.db) with 4 tables: patients, visits, summaries, audit_log.
           20 patients, each with one old approved visit.
    Why:   The recall card needs a visit to show.
    Files: db/seed.py     Run it: python db/seed.py   (it makes a fresh database each time)

S3  The AI summary
    What:  Each transcript is sent to the AI model (Groq, openai/gpt-oss-120b, temperature 0).
           The answer is a JSON file with the fields of the summary.
    Why:   This is the main job of the tool. Temperature 0 and a fixed model make a run
           repeatable (a second run gave the same medicines and doses on 18 of 20).
    Files: summary_service/summarize.py, compare_runs.py, prompt_v1.txt (and v2, v3)

S4  The scoring script
    What:  A script that compares each AI summary with the expected summary and gives a
           score. Test of the scorer: a perfect copy scores 20 of 20, a copy with one
           dose removed scores 19 of 20.
    Why:   We cannot read 20 summaries by hand after every change.
    Files: summary_service/score_t1.py

S5  First T1 result and one prompt fix
    What:  T1 = how many of 20 summaries keep six details: medicine, dose, frequency,
           duration, symptom, history. run1 with the first prompt: 8 of 20. We read the
           failed summaries and found the causes (Hindi words copied, "no ..." symptoms and
           past history left out, two medicines dropped). We added lines to the prompt
           (prompt_v3) and run2 scored 16 of 20.
    Why:   To see how far we are from 18 of 20, and if the prompt can fix it.
    Gate:  Below 14 after the fix would mean more prompt work. 16 is above 14.
    Files: summary_service/prompt_v3.txt, results/results_sheet.csv

S6  The medicine and dose checker
    What:  For every medicine of a summary: is the name in a reference list? Is the dose one
           of the usual doses of that medicine? If not, flag it (with a "did you mean ..."
           suggestion for names). Result on the real summaries: 10 of 10 planted errors
           flagged, 0 of 10 clean transcripts flagged by mistake.
    Why:   A wrong dose is the biggest danger. The checker does not use the expected summary,
           because the real app has none (Hitesh asked for this).
    Files: summary_service/check_medicines.py, formulary.csv (the list, with a source column)

S9  The recall card
    What:  Search a patient by name, see the last approved visit: date, main complaint,
           medicines with doses, follow-up advice. Test: 20 of 20 cards for the right
           patient, 80 of 80 fields correct.
    Why:   This is what saves the doctor's time.
    Files: app/recall.py, app/app.py, app/test_s9.py

S7  The review screen
    What:  Shows the summary and the flags. Approve is blocked until each flag is fixed
           (change the medicine) or dismissed ("it is fine").
    Why:   The doctor must see the doubtful items before approving.
    Files: app/review.py, app/pages/1_Review.py, app/test_s7.py

S8  Approve and save, and the audit log
    What:  Approve saves the visit. Leaving it unapproved saves nothing. Every action
           (approved, left unapproved, blocked) is written in the audit log with the time.
           Test: of 20 summaries, 15 approved and saved, 0 unapproved saved, 20 of 20 audit entries.
    Why:   The plan's rule: nothing is saved until the doctor approves. The log is the proof.
    Files: app/save.py, app/test_s8.py

S10 Timing sessions with 3 role-players
    What:  3 people play doctors. We time how long they need to find the last visit on the card,
           by reading the transcript, and to review and approve. Target: card median under 15 s,
           at least 2x faster than the transcript, approval under 30 s.
    Why:   The code tests say the tool works. Only people can say if it is fast.
    Files: app/timing.py, app/pages/2_Timing.py, app/timing_report.py, session_sheet.txt
    State: the code is ready. THE SESSIONS ARE NOT DONE YET.

S11 The final run of T1 to T5
    What:  T1 summary accuracy (18 of 20), T2 medicine-dose safety (10 of 10), T3 recall card
           (20 of 20), T4 review time (under 30 s), T5 Hindi-English (4 of 5). Run once on the
           final build. Nothing is changed after it.
    Why:   So the numbers do not come from an old version.
    Files: run_tests.py (one command, one table, saved in results/final_run.txt)

S12 The results note
    What:  One page: each test number against its target, the call (go, fix or stop), what we
           found, the limits. make_note.py fills the table from the real numbers.
    Why:   Hitesh and you decide from measured numbers, not from feelings.
    Files: make_note.py, results_note.txt

5. IDEAS YOU MUST BE ABLE TO EXPLAIN
------------------------------------
- Prompt: the written instruction we give the AI. We changed only the prompt between run1 and run2.
- Temperature 0: the AI gives (almost) the same answer each time. Needed for repeatable tests.
- Expected summary: the right answer, used ONLY to give marks. The real app never sees it.
- Planted error: a mistake we put in on purpose (a wrong medicine name or dose) to see if the
  checker finds it. The AI must copy what the doctor said; the checker flags it.
- Flag: "doctor, please look at this". It does not decide anything. The doctor decides.
- Audit log: a list of everything that happened, with the time.
- Median: the middle number. One very slow person does not spoil it.
- Gate: a number that decides if we go on (T1 14 of 20 after the prompt fix).


6. QUESTIONS PEOPLE MAY ASK, AND HONEST ANSWERS
-----------------------------------------------
Q: Why is T1 only 16 of 20?
A: The summaries still miss some past history, symptoms or a frequency in 4 transcripts
   (T11, T12, T18, T19). The plan calls 16 or 17 "inconclusive: fix and run once more".

Q: Is the prompt tuned only for these 20 transcripts?
A: Yes, it was improved by reading the failures of these 20, so on new transcripts the score
   may be lower. We used examples in the prompt that are not from the test data.

Q: Where does the medicine list come from?
A: It was written from general knowledge and checked against public drug labels and sites
   (Drugs.com, FDA and UK labels and similar). It was not reviewed by a doctor or pharmacist.
   For a real product we need a verified drug database.

Q: What does the checker NOT catch?
A: A correct name with a correct dose that is the wrong medicine for this patient. It also
   checks one tablet strength, not the total dose of the day.

Q: Why does the card test (S9) pass so easily?
A: The database was made from the same expected summaries, so it shows the lookup works.
   It does not measure how good the AI summary is. T1 does that.

Q: Why do we need 3 people for S10?
A: The code cannot tell if a human is faster. 3 people is the plan's number. It is a first
   sign, not a proof.

Q: What if the real app has no expected summary?
A: That is the point: the checker uses a reference list, not the expected summary.


7. LIMITS (say them yourself before someone asks)
-------------------------------------------------
- 20 mock transcripts, 3 role-players, no real patients or recordings.
- The prompt was tuned on the same 20 transcripts.
- The medicine list is a demo list.
- T1's word rules for frequency, symptom and history are simple.
- There is no script that checks the data for phone numbers (we removed that check in S2); the
  data was written by hand without real names or phones.
- Streamlit screens are for a demo, not for many users.
